How it works · scroll to play

From a message to a measurement, in one lab period.

Follow one transmission from the first click to the final error count.

STEP 1 — SIGNAL

Pick what to send.

Choose audio, an image, text or a test wave. Set shape, frequency and amplitude, and preview the message before anything leaves the PC.

Sine · 1000 Hz · A 0.9 · 1 s
STEP 2 — MODULATE

Choose a scheme and see the spectrum.

Change the modulation index or deviation and watch the sidebands move around the real carrier frequency.

FM · Δf 2.5 kHz · β = 2.25
STEP 3 — OVER THE AIR

Transmit through a real channel.

The signal leaves the antenna at 915 MHz and crosses the room. Swap antennas, change the distance, and see fading and noise for yourself.

TX 915 MHz · 3 MS/s
STEP 4 — RECEIVE

Capture and decode automatically.

The second station finds the packet, checks header and payload CRC, and rebuilds the image, sound or text. Bit errors are shown, never hidden.

Header CRC-16 OK · Payload CRC-32 checked
STEP 5 — MEASURE

Compare results side by side.

Every reception against the original: correlation, BER, PSNR, RX power, PAPR and bandwidth in one table, ready for the lab report.

About us

We make radio something students can see.

Aletheis Technologies builds teaching equipment for communication engineering. The name comes from aletheia, the Greek word for truth or disclosure: showing what is really there.

That is the idea behind the Wireless Communication Trainer. In most labs, the radio is a black box and the results arrive as a single number. The Wireless Communication Trainer opens the box: every stage from message to modulation to the received bits is on screen, measured, and open to question.

Every kit we ship is tested before it leaves us, and every request from a lab reaches the engineers who designed it.

Show, don't hide

Errors, noise and bad CRCs are part of the lesson, so the software reports them plainly.

Keep improving

The software gets regular updates with fixes and new features. Customers get them too.

Feedback from teachers and students shapes every software update.
